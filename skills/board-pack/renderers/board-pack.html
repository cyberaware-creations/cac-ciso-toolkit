Q3 2026 — SPECIMEN, not a real pack · Board · as at 2026-07-31

One thread runs through every section of this pack, and it is not the one we funded. What we funded improved: risks above appetite fell from three to two, and detection is producing the effect it was paid for. Third-party exposure did not. It appears here four times in four forms — as the framework outcome we have not started, because there is no supply-chain risk management programme and that governs what every other control is worth; as the single largest residual risk on the register; as the one of our four accepted risks and exceptions that passed its review date in April and was never re-confirmed; and, in July, as an incident. The vendor holding the customer records we accepted we do not control was breached, we determined it material on 14 July, and we filed on 17 July. The direction on everything else is right. The direction on this one is that a risk the board accepted became a risk the board disclosed, and the programme that would have managed it still has no owner.

Framework posture

6 items · as at 2026-07-26

Nine outcomes are in scope this cycle and eight of them have been assessed, so this is a partial picture and should be read as one. Of what we have looked at, six fall short of the target we set. The distance is not evenly spread. Two sit at Not Achieved against a target we chose deliberately, which means the work has not started rather than that it is going slowly: encryption of data at rest, and supply-chain risk management. A third — our risk-assessment outcome — carries a full-size gap for a different reason, that we have not assessed it yet, and it would be wrong to read that as a finding. The remaining three are under way rather than unstarted — one sits a single step from target and two are two steps away — and are the most likely to close inside the next cycle. We are asking for one decision today: fund the encryption programme, or record that the board accepts data-at-rest exposure for another two quarters.

Outcomes short of target

PR.DS-01
Data at rest and in transit is at Not Achieved against a target of Fully Achieved — the widest gap in the scope, and the one most likely to be asked about first after an incident, because it decides whether a stolen disk is an inconvenience or a notification.
GV.SC-01
There is no supply-chain risk management programme, and that governs what every other control here is worth: a standard we hold ourselves to and do not hold a vendor to is a standard with a gap in it.
ID.RA-01
This outcome has not been assessed yet, so it shows the full distance to target. That is a gap in what we know, not a finding about the control — assessing it is the work, and it should not be read alongside the two outcomes we did assess and found absent.
GV.RM-01
Risk management objectives are written but not yet agreed with the leadership who own the trade-offs, so the register records our judgement rather than the organisation's.
PR.AA-01
Identity and credential management is two steps short of target: the practice is in place but not applied consistently across services and hardware, which leaves the exceptions as the attack path.
GV.OC-01
Organisational mission is understood and already informs risk decisions. This is the closest to target and needs consolidation rather than investment.
Cyber Aware CreationsRisk

Risk

7 items · as at 2026-07-26

Two of our eleven tracked risks sit above the appetite the board set, down from three at the Q3 review — the direction is right, but the composition changed. Ransomware exposure through the internet-facing VPN appliance came down after an emergency patch and is now within appetite. Payment fraud moved the other way: two further supplier-invoice fraud attempts landed this quarter and email authentication is still not enforcing, so that risk rose from High to Critical and now sits alongside the third-party CRM exposure. Two accepted risks passed their re-validation date and need the board to either renew or withdraw the acceptance, and two risks are past their scheduled review. We are asking for two decisions today: fund DMARC enforcement to close the payment-fraud path, and renew or withdraw the two lapsed acceptances.

Risks

R-003
A breach at the CRM vendor would expose customer records we do not directly control. The insurance rider moves the financial loss but not the regulatory notification duty or the customer-facing consequence, so this stays above appetite regardless of the transfer.
R-007
An attacker impersonating a supplier can redirect a live payment before anyone notices. Two attempts landed this quarter; callback verification caught both, but that control is a person on a phone, not a system. Until email authentication enforces, the next attempt only has to beat one person having a bad day.
R-006
An unpatched internet-facing appliance is the most direct route to enterprise-wide ransomware. The emergency patch closed the immediate path and brought this within appetite; the network segmentation that would keep it there is still unfunded.
R-008
Regulated customer data can leave through unmanaged endpoints with no detective control in the path, and the risk has no named owner — which means nobody is accountable for closing it.
R-001
Stolen administrator credentials are the difference between an incident and an outage. Privileged access management has already cut the blast radius; this is now a managed risk rather than an open one.

arrangements

VA-001
Production hosting for the plant historian. A day of lost output is roughly a week of aftermarket margin, and the exit strategy has been written but never exercised — so the plan to leave is a document, not a demonstrated capability. The assessment is also past its annual cadence.
VA-002
CRM hosting. The dependency traces to a system the business calls critical, but the arrangement owner has assigned a lower level on the basis that renewals continue offline for a fortnight. That disagreement is recorded rather than resolved: the trace and the owner are looking at different things, and both are on the page.
VA-003
The marketing attribution sandbox supports nothing we have declared, so its criticality is untraced rather than low. A fourth party was introduced into its chain in June without notice, which is the kind of change a low-criticality arrangement has no cadence to catch.
VA-005
Replacement warehouse routing, opened when the Tailspin arrangement was retired in March. The predecessor's data return and deletion are closed and recorded against it. This one has not yet been traced to a workflow.
Cyber Aware Creationsai

ai

5 items · as at 2026-08-07

Five AI deployments run across four systems, and the two facts worth the board's time are both about change rather than about models. Contoso re-based the assistant that screens job applicants onto a different foundation model in June and left the product version number unchanged; every answer in our April assessment was about a different model, and nothing except this register noticed. Separately, a writing tool nobody approved is drafting customer-facing proposals — found in a CASB review in May, in this register the same day, and still in use. Neither is a failure of a control. Both are the same gap: things change under us faster than the cycle that looks at them.

deployments

D-001
Screens job applicants and produces the decision, on regulated personal data. It is the most assessed thing here — a red-team report, a signed no-training commitment, controls recorded against integrity and privacy — and the assessment was made against a base model the provider has since replaced. What we know about it is real and is now a quarter out of date.
D-002
Drafts marketing copy on the same assistant. A person edits everything before it leaves, so it is the one deployment here where the low level is the whole story.
D-003
Scores churn for the renewals desk, who work the list every morning. It has not been assessed since June last year, which is past the annual cadence for its level.
D-004
Auto-remediates low-severity tickets without a person in the loop. It was assessed in March against two connected systems; it reaches three now, and the third is the change queue. What a deployment can reach is what bounds the damage, so it was assessed as a smaller thing than the one running today.
D-005
Drafts customer-facing proposals on a tool nobody sanctioned, found in a CASB egress review in May. Nobody has declared what it supports, so its criticality is untraced rather than low — we cannot yet say what would be affected if it were wrong, and it is writing to customers.

Metrics

6 items · as at 2026-07-31

Six numbers are tracked; three are past the line we set for them and two of those are still moving the wrong way. The pattern is not random. Patching and phishing both worsened this quarter, and they are the two halves of the same route in — a message that gets clicked and a machine that has not been fixed. Identity moved the other way and is now effectively complete, and attacker dwell time is down by three days, so the detection investment is showing. The one that should concern the board most is not the worst number but the oldest: we last tested a backup restore in November, at 40% success, and have not measured it since. That is eight months without evidence on the control that decides whether an incident is a bad week or a business-continuity failure. We are asking for two decisions: fund the patching backlog, and schedule a restore test before the next review.

Metrics

M-006
We last tested backup restores in November, when 40% succeeded, and we have not measured since — eight months without evidence on the control that decides whether a ransomware incident is recoverable. This is a gap in what we know, not a claim that recovery has failed.
M-001
Critical patches applied inside our own service level fell from 93% to 88%, crossing the 90% line the board agreed. The gap is concentrated in internet-facing systems, which is the population where the number matters most.
M-003
Simulated phishing click rate rose from 4.1% to 6.8%, past our 6% warning line. Combined with the patching slip above, this is the same attack path getting easier at both ends.
M-002
Median attacker dwell time came down from 11 days to 8. The detection work is producing the effect it was funded for, and this is the clearest return in the set.
M-004
Privileged accounts covered by multi-factor authentication reached 99%, up from 96%. The remaining accounts are service accounts with a documented plan, not oversights.
M-005
We blocked 2.14 million malicious emails this quarter. This is a volume figure, not a risk figure — it measures how much arrived, not how much got through, and it should not be read as reassurance.

Accepted risks and exceptions

4 items · as at 2026-07-31

We formally accept two residual risks and operate two documented exceptions to our own standards. Three of the four are on schedule. The one that is not is the CRM vendor acceptance, which the board approved last October and which passed its review date in April — four months carrying a decision nobody has re-confirmed. That is the item to deal with today, and the choice is binary: re-validate it on the record, or withdraw the acceptance and fund a different answer. The MFA exception in Finance comes up for review in ten days and expires at the end of November, so it will return to this table either way. Both exceptions have a compensating control in place and named, which is the difference between an exception and a gap.

Accepted risks

A-002
The board accepted in October that customer records sit with our CRM vendor beyond our direct control, on the basis of contractual terms and the insurance rider. That acceptance was due for re-confirmation in April and has not been re-confirmed, so we are four months into carrying a decision on paper that nobody has re-tested against the current facts.
A-001
We accept a forty-day patch window on nine internet-facing systems because the vendor ships quarterly. Re-validated in July against verified virtual patching, and next due in January.

Exceptions

X-001
Finance operates without phishing-resistant MFA until the ERP upgrade lands, offset by out-of-band callback verification on every payment change above ten thousand. Due for review in ten days and expiring at the end of November.
X-002
The legacy OT segment runs without endpoint detection because the controller operating system cannot host an agent. It is offset by network segmentation and full flow capture at the boundary, and the replacement sits in the 2027 capital plan.
Cyber Aware CreationsIncidents

Incidents

4 items · as at 2026-07-31

Three incidents were open in this period and one earlier matter was closed. The significant one is the residual risk this board accepted in October: customer records sitting with our CRM vendor beyond our direct control. That acceptance fell due for re-confirmation in April and was not re-confirmed; in July the vendor was breached. It was determined material on 14 July and reported on Form 8-K on 17 July, inside the required window. A four-hour degradation of card authorisation at the EU payments subsidiary was classified as major under DORA and reported to the national competent authority, and was separately determined not material for securities purposes — two different questions with two different tests. The third, a renewed wave of credential-stuffing against the customer portal, is still under assessment and is being assessed together with the two earlier waves rather than on its own.

Incidents

I-001
An earlier wave of the same credential-stuffing activity was determined not material in March and closed after credential resets and tighter rate limiting; it is retained as the reference point for the wave now under assessment.

I-003
